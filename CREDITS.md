Unciv VR is a VR frontend for [Unciv](https://github.com/yairm210/Unciv), the open-source Civilization V remake by
Yair Morgenstern and the Unciv contributors. Unciv VR's own code (the Godot client in `client/`) is licensed
under the Mozilla Public License 2.0 (see [LICENSE](LICENSE)), the same licence as Unciv. The game rules, AI and turn processing are Unciv's own, run by the
bridge in our Unciv fork (`unciv/bridge`). That code is under the Mozilla Public License 2.0, like the rest of Unciv.

## The low-poly tileset

The 3D tiles in `client/tilesets/LowPoly` are Unciv VR's own, generated by `client/tools/make_lowpoly_tileset.gd`,
and licensed under the Mozilla Public License 2.0 like the rest of the client. The way tiles pick their pieces
copies Unciv's tileset system, and the choice of which natural wonders get their own look was helped by looking at
Unciv's FantasyHex tileset and [UnTile](https://github.com/Malwen/UnTile-Civ6-Tileset) by Malwen (MIT); no
images from either are used.

## Backgrounds and tables

The backgrounds and tables in `client/backgrounds` and `client/tables` are Unciv VR's own, made by the scripts in
`client/tools` (`make_surroundings.gd`, `lowpoly_backgrounds.gd`, `tables.gd`, `make_mountain_king.gd`) and
licensed under the Mozilla Public License 2.0 like the rest of the client - with one exception.

**Webb Deep Field** (`client/backgrounds/WebbDeepField/sky.jpg`) is an all-round sky adapted from the James Webb
Space Telescope's First Deep Field (SMACS 0723). Credit: **NASA, ESA, CSA, STScI**. NASA and STScI release Webb
images for free use with credit, and ESA/Webb material is licensed
[CC BY 4.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/). The image has been adapted into an equirectangular
panorama and re-encoded as a JPEG to keep the repository small; the credit travels with it, in the background's `author` field and here. It is the only third-party
media file kept in this repository - everything else is loaded at runtime from the Unciv checkout.

## Unciv's icons

The client shows Unciv's own icons so Unciv players feel at home. They aren't copied into this repository: the
client loads them at runtime from the Unciv checkout next to it (`unciv/android/Images.*`). The icons used are:
- policies and policy branches
- technologies
- stats
- resources and improvements
- unit types and units
- buildings
- nations and city-state types
- tile icons, including the map-overlay toggles

These images are by many authors, mostly from [the Noun Project](https://thenounproject.com). They are licensed
under CC BY 3.0, CC BY 4.0, CC BY-SA 3.0/4.0, CC0 or public domain. The full list of authors and licences is in
Unciv's [docs/Credits.md](https://github.com/yairm210/Unciv/blob/master/docs/Credits.md), which is also in
`unciv/docs/Credits.md` here.

**Changes we make:** the client resizes the icons, and many are tinted and set on coloured round badges at runtime.
Examples are unit flags in civ colours, nation emblems, and resource and improvement markers. The original files
are not changed.

## Unciv's sound effects

The client plays Unciv's own sound effects (`unciv/android/assets/sounds`), loaded the same way as the icons:
read when the game runs, never copied into this repository. They come mostly from
[freesound.org](https://freesound.org) and [soundbible.com](https://soundbible.com) under CC0, CC BY 3.0 or
CC BY 4.0. Authors and licences are listed in the "Sound credits" section of Unciv's Credits.md.

**Changes we make:** none to the files. The client chooses which sound to play where Unciv would, sets how
loud it is, and in VR plays it from the spot on the table it belongs to.

Unciv's music ("Thatched Villagers" by Kevin MacLeod, CC BY 4.0) is not part of Unciv's repository - Unciv
downloads it separately - and the client doesn't play music.

## Unciv's translations

The game's languages are Unciv's own translation files (`unciv/android/assets/jsons/translations`), made by Unciv's
translators and part of Unciv (Mozilla Public License 2.0). They're read when the game runs; the Quest app carries
them in its packed data.

The frontend catalogs in `client/translations/` reuse matching wording from Unciv's translators under MPL 2.0.
New VR-specific translations are AI-assisted drafts maintained in this repository and awaiting native-speaker
review. The Arabic and Hebrew game-content additions are also drafts; see `client/translations/CONTENT.md`.
